--- the blinks are not in the signal ---
Not a threshold problem. Sweeping blink_z from 12 down to 4 yields zero blink
detections at every value, and nothing is rejected by any gate
(rejections: ratio 0, emg 0, slow 0) - the state machine never sees a candidate.

Not a baseline problem either. The blink feature avoids a high-pass by design
(to suppress the undershoot echo), which costs noise floor on drifty data: its
baseline MAD is 2491 counts against 589 for a plain 0.5-8 Hz bandpass. Adding a
high-pass back reduces the spread to 1722 / 728 / 347 counts at 0.3 / 0.5 /
0.8 Hz, and still produces at most 2 blink events. The excursions simply are
not there.

Peak-picking the frontal envelope over the whole recording finds 18 events above
z=4. Only about six fall in the 28 s blink block, irregularly spaced, and two in
the held block. Ten blinks at 3 s spacing would be unmissable.

Blink amplitude is if anything slightly larger than in the good session. The

But amplitude alone does not explain ten missing blinks, because the jaw
clenches came through cleanly on the same two electrodes. Jaw is detected from
the 25-110 Hz EMG band, which couples through mediocre skin contact; a blink is
a slow EOG dipole that needs the pad to sit close to the eye with proper
coupling. Electrodes that have ridden up toward the hairline keep picking up
temporalis EMG while losing the eye dipole almost entirely. That matches what
was seen on 09-18-27, where FP1 carried ongoing EEG yet showed 0.2-1.5 MADs of
blink-band excursion against FP2's 11-60.
